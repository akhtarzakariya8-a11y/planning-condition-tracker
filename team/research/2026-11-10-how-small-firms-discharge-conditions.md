# How small planning consultancies and architects handle discharge of conditions, and what they charge

Scout run, 10 Nov 2026. Question taken from the top of the "Research agenda" in `team/MARKET.md`.

## The question

How do small planning consultancies and architects handle discharge of conditions today,
step by step, and what do they charge clients for it? (Sets what our time saving is worth.)

## The answer in three sentences

Every small firm page read describes the same manual job (read the notice, sort conditions by
trigger, commission specialists, check each report against the condition wording, bundle what
is ready into one application, chase the officer), and none mentions any software beyond a
spreadsheet. Firms almost never publish a discharge price: one of eleven firm pages gives a
figure (a pack "from £345"), the rest quote a fixed price on request, and discharge is usually
billed as an extra outside the planning-stage fee. The paperwork our tool speeds up is a small
part of the client's bill next to specialist reports (£300 to £1,500 each, contamination
£2,000 to £10,000 or more), and three of the six 2025 to 2026 firm pages that state the council
fee get it wrong, so the defensible value is speed and accuracy at the quoting and scoping
step rather than a lower total cost.

## Evidence

### 1. The step-by-step process small firms describe (all pages read 10 Nov 2026)

Eleven firm pages were read: four planning consultancies, three architecture or drawing
practices, two specialist consultancies, and two developer or project-delivery firms. They
agree on the sequence:

| Step | What the firms say | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Read the notice, sort by trigger | Pre-commencement, pre-occupation, compliance. "Initial audit and strategy: identifies true pre-commencement conditions" | Approved Planning (17 Aug 2026); Unda (14 Jul 2026); rcbGroup (2026); DrawingsDirect (2026) |
| 2. Commission specialists | Ecology, drainage, trees, contamination. Seasonal limits: some bat, newt and bird surveys only in season (Arbtech, 21 May 2026); infiltration testing only November to May, and missing it "can add 6 to 12 months" (Unda) | Arbtech; Unda |
| 3. Check reports match the condition wording | "Vague reports are a common cause of rejection by planning officers" | Approved Planning |
| 4. Bundle into one application | One fee covers several conditions if their information is ready at the same time | Unda, Urbanist, Approved Planning, Rubix, Kisiel, rcbGroup |
| 5. Submit | Planning Portal, or the council's own system (Lambeth runs a pilot digital service with "bugs and issues may occur") | Lambeth (2026) |
| 6. Liaise and negotiate | Respond to consultees; for drainage the Lead Local Flood Authority "often decides the outcome" | Unda; Approved Planning; Rubix |
| 7. Chase | Refund at 12 weeks; deemed discharge notice from 6 weeks (England) | Urbanist; Unda; rcbGroup |

Tools: no firm page mentions software for this job. The only tool suggested is "a simple
spreadsheet listing each condition, the action needed, specialist reports, consultant quotes
and report dates" (Kisiel, 4 Aug 2021). RTPI practice advice for new sole practitioners lists
only bookkeeping, time-tracking and email marketing tools (RTPI, April 2016).

Who does the work varies. Two specialist firms (Unda for drainage, Arbtech for ecology and
trees) market discharge directly to developers, so the planner or architect is not always the
one submitting. TCPA Ltd (Cardiff) offers to take on condition management for architects and
planning consultants.

### 2. What they charge

**Published prices are rare.** Of eleven firm pages, one publishes a discharge price:

- DrawingsDirect (MCIAT technologists, London and Essex homes): "We prepare the pack from £345."
  Fixed quote promised "within 60 minutes" of an enquiry. (2026 page, fees stated from 1 Apr 2026.)
- 4D Planning (London and Manchester planning consultancy, 2026 fee page): no discharge price;
  desktop advice £275 an hour, a one-hour site visit £500, planning applications £875 to £5,000.
- Urbanist Architecture (London RIBA practice): minimum engagement £3,000 (page of 16 Jan 2025).
- Unda and Arbtech: "fixed-fee quotes" or "fixed-price quotes", no figures. Rubix Planning,
  TCPA Ltd, rcbGroup, Approved Planning, Kisiel: no figures, contact for a quote or free advice.

**Discharge sits outside the planning-stage fee.** A self-builder's architect listed further
fees for "re-submission, appeal and building regs", with discharge not in the quote, then
asked about £1,000 more after permission (Buildhub, 3 Dec 2019). Crown Architecture's 2026
guide says supporting documents "carry additional professional fees". One Buildhub poster,
undated, wrote: "The architect practice wanted £15k to work on these along with the Building
Control submission" for 14 conditions, and did the work themselves (UNVERIFIED date; the
figure includes Building Control work).

**Hourly rates behind any quote:**

- Architects (RIBA Journal, Fees Bureau survey, latest edition, year not stated on the page):
  a quarter of sole principals charge £99 an hour or more, another quarter no more than £63;
  associates average £75 in 3 to 5 person practices and £98 in practices of 30 or more;
  architects with under five years' experience £55 and £71.
- Planning consultants: £75 and £100 an hour in two self-builders' accounts (Buildhub, 27 Apr
  2020), a quoted range of £30 to £250, and a suggested £300 an hour for a review (Sep 2025);
  4D Planning £275 an hour (2026). RTPI practice advice gives day rates "from as little as £150
  to £2,000 upwards" (April 2016).
- RIBA's guide for homeowners (2026) names three fee methods (percentage of build cost, lump
  sum, time charge) and does not mention discharge of conditions at all.

**What councils charge for advice on discharge** (a price clients already pay per condition):

- Southwark (charges from 1 Apr 2024, including VAT): minor schemes (1 to 6 homes) £419.40 per
  condition for a meeting, site visit and advice letter, follow-up £239.70; strategic schemes
  £599.20 per condition or obligation for a bespoke discharge service.
- Haringey (including VAT, undated schedule): £498 fixed for advice on a discharge of condition
  (householder, minor, telecoms); Planning Performance Agreements for discharge "start at £1,000
  per condition".

**What the specialist reports cost** (the bulk of the client's bill):
arboricultural £300 to £1,000; ecology £600 to £1,200; flood risk, drainage, highways,
archaeology £600 to £1,500 each; contamination £1,000 initial, possibly "a further £10,000"
(self-build.co.uk, 2026). A Phase 1 plus Phase 2 contamination investigation on a domestic site
£2,000 to £8,000 (rcbGroup, 2026). Archaeology "could cost tens of thousands" (Homebuilding &
Renovating, 31 Mar 2021).

### 3. Professionals get the council fee wrong

The council fee in England has been £309 (other) and £89 (householder) per application since
1 Apr 2026 (Planning Portal). Of the six firm pages from 2025 or 2026 that state it:

| Page | Date | Fee stated | Right? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unda | 14 Jul 2026 | £309 / £89 | Yes |
| DrawingsDirect | 2026 | £309 / £89 | Yes |
| Crown Architecture | 2026 | £89 | Yes |
| Approved Planning | 17 Aug 2026 | £145 / £43 "as of recent fee updates" | No (the Dec 2023 figures) |
| rcbGroup | 2026 | "£34 per condition" | No (2019 figure, and wrong unit) |
| Urbanist Architecture | 16 Jan 2025 | £116 / £34 | No (already stale when written) |

Three of six are wrong, and one says the fee is per condition. The fee rises again on
8 Dec 2026 if Parliament approves (see `team/EVIDENCE.md`).

### 4. The agent market is many small players

West Suffolk Council publishes how often each agent's applications are valid on receipt. For
1 Sep 2020 to 30 Aug 2021 it listed 70 agents with five or more applications; the median agent
made 10 applications to that council in the year, the busiest 60; the median agent had about
67% valid on receipt, ranging from 11% to 100% (all application types, not discharge alone;
data four years old).

### 5. Evidence against the team's current beliefs

- **"They bill by the hour, so a drafted submission saves billable time"** (`team/MARKET.md`
  section 4). Firms mostly quote fixed prices for this job. On fixed-fee work, saved time is
  margin. On hourly work, saved time cuts the firm's income, so "saves you hours" is a weak pitch.
- **Many self-builders do it themselves.** "Discharging pre-commencement conditions is mostly
  paperwork, you can do much of it yourself" (Buildhub, 17 Jan 2019); the 10-condition and
  14-condition posters both did their own. The smallest clients may not pay anyone for this step.
- **The tool does not touch the biggest cost.** Specialist reports and seasonal survey windows
  drive both cost and delay. Aftergrant can only make sure they are commissioned early enough.
- **The submitter is often not a planner.** Drainage and ecology firms sell discharge directly,
  so they may be a channel or a competitor, not only a "specialist to hire".

## What it means for what we build

1. **Pitch quoting speed and fixed-fee margin, not "billable hours saved".** Firms quote after
   reading the notice ("fixed quote within 60 minutes"; "free initial advice"). Aftergrant already
   produces the inputs for that quote: number of applications, council fees, specialists, timing.
   HYPOTHESIS until a consultant confirms it.
2. **A quote scope sheet** (proposed today in `team/BACKLOG.md`): one page a consultant can use to
   price the job: conditions to discharge, batches, council fees before and after 8 Dec, specialists
   to commission, and seasonal flags. This is the smallest form of ticket 14.
3. **Fee accuracy is a visible edge.** Half the professional pages read are wrong on the fee or the
   unit. The landing page (ticket 4) should state "per application, not per condition" with the
   date and source.
4. **Price arithmetic (not a forecast):** if one discharge pack sells from £345, a £29 a month plan is
   under a tenth of one pack and £79 a month under a quarter. A firm doing two packs a month would
   spend 4% to 11% of that revenue on the tool. HYPOTHESIS that this is affordable to them.
5. **Specialist firms are worth testing as a second channel.** They already market discharge and
   need the condition wording exactly; the Consultant Scoping output is close to what they need.
6. **Do not build** a cost estimate for specialist reports: figures vary by region and site, and
   the sources are guides, not price lists.

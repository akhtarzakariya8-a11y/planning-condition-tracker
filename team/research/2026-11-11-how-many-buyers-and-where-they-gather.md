# How many small planning consultancies, small architects and SME developers there are, and where they gather

Scout run, 11 Nov 2026. Question taken from the top open item of the "Research agenda" in
`team/MARKET.md` (no question from Zak was waiting).

## The question

How many small planning consultancies and SME developers are there in England, and where do
they gather (bodies, forums, newsletters, events)? (Size of the market and how to reach it.)

## The answer in three sentences

Nobody counts firms directly, but official and trade-body figures put each buyer group in the
low thousands: about 2,400 self-employed planners in the UK (about 2,000 in England, 2019),
about 3,000 RIBA chartered practices with fewer than 10 staff (2024) and about 2,500 active
housebuilders (2024), so £50k a year at £79 a month needs roughly 1 in 150 of these firms to pay.
They gather in a few places: the RTPI Independent Consultants' Network for sole planners, the
Federation of Master Builders and HBF for small builders, and the Planning Portal's webinars for
everyone who submits. The Planning Portal, which handles over 90% of applications, now sells AI
and workflow tools to the same people, and adds a service charge of up to £99 to non-householder
discharge applications made through it, which our fee figures leave out.

### 2. Small architecture and technology practices

- **RIBA chartered practices, 2024**: the size bands in the Business Benchmarking 2024 summary
  table add up to about 3,700 practices and 40,800 staff. About 81% have fewer than 10 staff
  (about 3,000 practices); 27% are one person. Private housing is 27% of all revenue, more than
  half for practices under 10 staff, and 80% for one-person practices.
- **Average profit per practice, 2024**: £26,000 (one person), £39,000 (two), £61,000 (three
  to five), £95,000 (five to ten). Small practices "were the most challenged" over the year.
  The summary does not mention software or IT spend.
- **Architects on the ARB register**: 38,981 in 2024 (ARB 2025 year in review; 2025 figure is
  in the full report, not read).
- **CIAT (architectural technologists)**: the Annual Review 2024-25 gives no total. A survey
  with 379 responses is called "approximately 4% of the membership", which implies about
  9,500 members (derived, UNVERIFIED). Technologists do much householder and minor work.

Survey evidence on the pain:
- **HBF State of Play 2025/26** (225 SME respondents, 21 May to 18 June 2025; 62% build 1 to 50
  homes a year): 94% name "delays in securing planning permission / discharging conditions"
  as a barrier; 89% local authority resources. "Conditions sign off" is listed among planning
  constraints. The survey went to HBF members and to clients of Travis Perkins and Close
  Brothers Property Finance.
- **FMB House Builders' Survey** (169 builders of up to 100 homes; release 16 Dec 2025): 47%
  name planning as their single greatest constraint; 64% "feel certain about planning
  outcomes"; conditions are not mentioned in the release.
- **LSE London, Supporting SME Housebuilders** (Nov 2024; 61 respondents, summer 2024): 67%
  build 1 or 2 to 3 homes a year; 98% had used no government support.
- **CMA**: the planning system is "the biggest barrier" SMEs face, and decision times affect
  their ability to raise finance (no passage on conditions found in the part read).

### 5. The Planning Portal is moving into this space (found while answering)

- **SmartApply** launched 3 Sep 2026: AI planning guidance ("Ask Navi"), constraints shown during
  the application, bulk uploads with a live checklist, "enhanced completeness and readiness
  checks" due October 2026, Local Validation Checklists later, and a hand-over to Building
  Control. "Project Workspaces" (related applications, people and information together) is
  planned "over the coming months". Nothing covers decision notices, conditions or discharge.
- **Service charge bands from 3 Sep 2026**: Band 0 £0, Band 1 £99 inc VAT, Band 2 £149 inc VAT.
  The FAQ says the charge applies only to applications with a planning fee over £100. The
  banding guide puts discharge of conditions in "Band 0 or Band 1 – depends on development
  type/scale". So today a non-householder discharge made through the Portal costs about
  £309 + £99 = £408, and £534 if the £435 fee starts on 8 Dec 2026. Householder discharge is
  £89 today (under the £100 threshold); at £125 it would cross it. Whether the Portal will then
  charge householder discharges is UNVERIFIED.
- The Portal "processes over 90% of all planning applications", sponsors the ICN conference and
  chaired its AI session. It already has the audience, the payment step and pricing power.

### 6. Evidence against the team's current beliefs

- **"Small developers suffer most" is real but not universal.** In the FMB survey 64% of small
  builders feel certain about planning outcomes and fewer than half (47%) name planning as their
  greatest constraint. HBF's 94% joins planning permission and discharge in one answer, so it does
  not show how much of the pain is conditions.
- **The developer pool is small and getting smaller.** About 2,500 active housebuilders, down from
  12,000 or more in 1988, and two-thirds of one small survey build three homes a year or fewer.
  Few permissions a year each supports the existing view that group 2 is price sensitive.
- **Small architects earn little.** Average profit of £26,000 for a one-person RIBA practice means
  £79 a month (£948 a year) is about 3.6% of profit; £29 a month is about 1.3%.
- **A much bigger player is building for the same users.** The Planning Portal has the users,
  the payment step and an AI roadmap. If Project Workspaces grows into post-decision tracking,
  it would compete with the tracker directly (HYPOTHESIS; nothing announced).

## Still unknown

- A direct count of planning consultancy firms in England by size. Only headcounts exist, and
  the firm-type split is from 2019. The ONS size-band tables by SIC code were not readable here,
  and planning consultancy has no SIC code of its own (it sits inside architectural activities).
- How many members the ICN has, what its conference costs, and whether it accepts supplier talks
  or sponsors in 2026 or 2027 (the 2025 sponsors suggest it does).
- What share of discharge applications go through the Planning Portal rather than straight to
  the council, and how the Portal will band householder discharge once the fee is £125.
- How many SME developers who are not housebuilders (conversions, commercial, one-off sites) get
  condition-heavy permissions. The Companies House count mixes these with single-site SPVs.
- Whether anyone in these groups would pay. Only conversations can answer this (MARKET.md section 6).

## What it means for what we build

1. **Size the target honestly.** Roughly 2,000 sole planners, about 3,000 small RIBA practices
   plus technologists, and about 2,500 housebuilders in the UK: under 10,000 firms in total.
   144 subscribers at £29 a month is about 1.5% to 2% of them; 53 at £79 is about 0.5% to 1%
   (arithmetic, not a forecast). Reachable, but only with a tool small firms find cheap.
2. **Show the full submission cost.** The fee panel shows the council fee only. Through the Planning
   Portal, a non-householder discharge application appears to pay up to £99 more (Band 1), so each
   extra application costs up to about £408 today, not £309. Proposed today as P3 in `team/BACKLOG.md`.
3. **Reach group 1 through the ICN first.** It is the one place built for sole and small planning
   practices, its conference already runs an AI tools session, and suppliers sponsor it. Zak, as a
   student, could ask to show the tool or join as a supporter (RED: contacting people is Zak's).
4. **Reach small builders through who they already use.** HBF reached SMEs through a builders'
   merchant and a development lender. FMB's conference had software exhibitors. Both support
   lenders as a channel (MARKET.md group 3) without proving it.
5. **Watch the Planning Portal every month.** Keep the edge where it is not: after the decision
   (gates, programme, discharge batching, drafting, tracking).
6. **Do not build for national consultancies.** They are a minority of planners, and the evidence
   about pain and price points to small firms.
